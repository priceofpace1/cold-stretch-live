What three seasons say

In 16,086 games since 2022, a top-50 team six or more points behind pace at halftime had lost 9.5 points and got 0.6 back. From there it scored at 101.6% of its pregame pace, across 831 team-games.

So a fair live total is the score plus a normal pace for the time left. There is an edge only when the book prices the cold to continue.

What each rule would have done

Every rule here runs on the same readings, so the rule is chosen on results and not by feel. Readings are 30 seconds apart since November 22 and about a minute apart before. One deviation is the usual spread of the three-minute average, about a point and a half for a game total. November 17 is measured against itself; every later slate uses only the slates before it. Scores and play-by-play come from ESPN. Lines are a sportsbook’s pregame and live numbers as ESPN publishes them. ESPN drops the live line at the final whistle, so a recorder saves it every 30 seconds while games are on.

Fair is the current score plus a rest-of-game estimate fitted on 16,086 Division I games, 2022–23 through December 2025. It knows nothing about injuries, foul trouble or who is on the floor. Signals are judged on a three-minute rolling average of the gap, measured in deviations: the usual spread of that average, re-estimated from every completed slate. A signal is logged once the average has stayed two deviations out for two minutes. This is information, not betting advice.
